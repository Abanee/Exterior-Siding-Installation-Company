Find The Siding That Fits Your Home.

Exterior cladding defines your home’s character, maintenance obligations, and weather resilience. We present siding not as bulk hardware, but as curated architectural mediums tailored to your design vision.

Cladding Medium 01

Architectural Insulated Vinyl

Modern insulated vinyl has evolved far beyond lightweight plastic panels of the past. Contemporary engineered profiles feature rigid foam backing, deep woodgrain embossing, and locked-in thermal resistance.

Visual Character

Clean horizontal shadow lines, crisp corners, matte low-gloss finishes.

Maintenance Profile

Near zero. Never requires painting, scraping, or chemical resealing.

Best Suited For

Homeowners seeking maximum thermal value and zero upkeep routines.

Key Trade-Offs

Requires precise expansion gaps; cannot be easily custom-repainted later.

Inquire About Vinyl Options
Cladding Medium 02

Engineered Fiber Cement

A robust composite of Portland cement, sand, and cellulose fibers. It delivers the authentic weight and deep shadow lines of painted lumber while offering non-combustible protection and termite immunity.

Visual Character

Substantial architectural depth, authentic milled wood shadow lines, smooth or cedar texture.

Maintenance Profile

Low to moderate. Factory baked-on finishes last 12–15 years before repainting.

Best Suited For

Severe weather zones, wildfire-prone areas, and high-end curb appeal.

Key Trade-Offs

Heavier material requiring specialized cutting tools and diamond blades.

Inquire About Fiber Cement
Cladding Medium 03

Western Red Cedar

Unmatched natural beauty and architectural prestige. Western Red Cedar contains natural extractives that resist decay, accompanied by an organic fragrance, sound-dampening density, and rich grain variability.

Visual Character

Warm organic grain, rich amber tones, bespoke transparent or semi-solid stains.

Maintenance Profile

Periodic care required. Restaining or sealing recommended every 4–6 years.

Best Suited For

Craftsman, Scandinavian contemporary, mountain modern, and accent elevations.

Key Trade-Offs

Premium material investment; requires ongoing UV and moisture protection.

Comparing the three core materials.

A practical homeowner comparison of visual character, ongoing maintenance, design flexibility, and real-world considerations.

Consideration Insulated Vinyl Fiber Cement Western Red Cedar
Appearance & Character Crisp, clean lines with matte low-sheen colors. Consistent appearance across large elevations. Authentic heavy lumber look with sharp, deep shadow lines. Very difficult to distinguish from real painted wood. Unmatched natural grain, authentic warmth, and bespoke stain depth that enhances architectural character.
Maintenance Expectations Zero painting. Occasional gentle wash with mild household detergent and garden hose. Low to moderate. Baked-on factory finish holds color 12–15 years before repainting is needed. Periodic stewardship. Regular restaining or oil finishing every 4–6 years to maintain color vibrancy.
Durability & Safety High impact resistance with insulated foam backing. Impervious to moisture and rot. Non-combustible Class A fire rating. Immune to termites, woodpeckers, and rot. Naturally decay-resistant due to thujaplicins; requires fire-resistant barrier in wildland zones.
Design Flexibility Standard horizontal lap, board & batten, and traditional shake patterns in pre-set color charts. Extensive styling: wide boards, vertical panel grids, mitered corners, and custom factory color matching. Maximum artisanal flexibility: bevel lap, tongue & groove, shiplap, and custom stain matching.

Architectural Guidance

Matching material to home architecture.

Every home silhouette suggests specific proportions, shadow lines, and material pairings. Here is how our specialists guide selection.

Modern & Contemporary

Geometric volumes benefit from wide-panel fiber cement or vertical cedar reveals with concealed trim and finsihes.

Suggested: Fiber Cement + Cedar Accents

Craftsman & Bungalow

Requires horizontal lap siding with substantial corner casings, flared eaves, and organic wood trim for house.

Suggested: Fiber Cement Lap or Cedar Shingle

Modern Farmhouse

Defined by tall vertical board and batten siding, high contrast black accents, and deep porch overhangs.

Suggested: Vertical Vinyl or Fiber Cement

Traditional Colonial

Balanced symmetrical elevations suited to classic 4-inch horizontal lap with dentil moldings and wide frieze boards.

Suggested: Insulated Vinyl or Smooth Lap
